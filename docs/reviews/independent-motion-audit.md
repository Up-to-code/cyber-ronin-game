## Finding ledger — priority order

### M1 — Slow locomotion does not maintain stance contact

**Classification:** confirmed source-level contact mismatch; recording severity not measured. **Evidence:** `game/rig/rig_visual.gd:172–189,284–288`; enemy speeds at `game/actors/enemy_2d.gd:7–8`. The gait's stance curve moves 50 local pixels over 55% of a 90.909-pixel distance cycle, correctly cancelling root motion only when motion_blend=1 and moving in facing direction. The target is then blended toward a stationary local foot by abs(speed)/300. At settled 42 px/s patrol, stance foot world drift is approximately 42×(1−0.14)=36.1 px/s; at 108 px/s approach it is 69.1 px/s. This is a geometric calculation from source, not a measured screen-space slide.

**Why it matters:** the body can travel while the supposed supporting foot travels with it, preventing grounded weight even though bone lengths are correct. **Minimal correction:** separate gait amplitude/style blending from ground-contact ownership; store each stance foot in world/surface coordinates or derive an exactly cancelling contact curve for each speed. Include signed travel relative to facing, and transition into a new support before changing contact. **Acceptance:** record patrol 42, approach 108, run 300, acceleration, braking and reversal; overlay world foot contacts and fixed ground marks. Across each authored stance interval the supporting sole stays at its ground mark within render sampling precision; swing foot visibly clears and returns. Do not freeze movement to conceal the problem.

### M2 — Attack motion and damage do not share an authored contact pose

**Classification:** confirmed source timing/reach mismatch; target-specific visual impact remains to be recorded. **Evidence:** player `:67,249–268,338,362`; rig `:197,218–231,279–280`. All melee damage occurs 90 ms after starting the action, while a 280 ms kick reaches its sine-pulse extension maximum at 140 ms. A 300 ms sword reaches the end of its swing interpolation at 180 ms (60% of duration); power at 288 ms. Damage queries use root-distance rectangles (kick reach 85; sword 118–150; power 210), independent of the rendered weapon's swept region. The 90 ms time can intentionally be an early contact; the defect is absence of a defined, demonstrated matching contact configuration, not a universal requirement that damage wait for maximum extension.

**Why it matters:** a launch can precede a convincing limb/weapon connection or occur beyond its visible reach. **Minimal correction:** preserve the combat contact clock initially and author anticipation/contact/follow-through/recovery keys around that clock, then make the hit volume explicitly correspond to the intended blade/foot sweep (or establish an intentional shockwave with its own cue). **Acceptance:** capture 60 fps plus a damage-event marker at near/mid/max reach, stationary and moving. Show preceding anticipation frame, exact damage frame and two following frames; identify the actual visible point/sweep causing each hit. Any deliberate extended attack requires a readable corresponding effect.

### M3 — Kick is a mirrored endpoint pulse, without a staged whole-body transfer

**Classification:** source-confirmed limited performance; suitability for weighted samurai acting is an art-direction judgment supported by the user's rejection. **Evidence:** rig `:218–225,267–275`. Kick uses one symmetric sine pulse for hip translation, torso lean and front-foot extension. Rear foot stays local (-10,-8); pelvis rotation remains zero. Both arms jump toward fixed retracted targets. Moving attacks can still travel at 96 px/s (`player:251`), carrying the support foot with the root. The recording shows the brown enemy's kick-like extension at **8.25–8.50 s** and a return toward upright by **8.625 s**; those frames establish silhouette/phase samples only, not force quality at normal speed.

**Why it matters:** limb extension alone does not establish chamber, supporting-leg load, pelvis turn, contact and retraction. **Minimal correction:** author 4–6 explicit side-view poses for chamber/contact/recoil/settle; give pelvis, torso, support heel and hands separate timed responsibility. Keep root travel legal while pinning or intentionally stepping the support. For a turning/roundhouse kick, add approved view replacements or drawn action frames: the present rigid single-view rectangles cannot convey foreshortening by joint placement alone. **Acceptance:** clean silhouette sheet at those phases, then real-time sequence both directions with no FX. Supporting knee stays on its intended side, boot/hip orientation expresses the selected kick, contact is readable, and recovery returns through a balanced pose.

### M4 — Jump/fall and landing bypass the useful anticipation/weight information

**Classification:** confirmed source omission and frame-supported pose repetition. **Evidence:** player `:388–403,433–446`; rig `:187–193,205–245`. Jump/fall have no distinct match branch: all airborne nonaction states use feet at hip+(19,39) and hip+(-17,43), the same lean offset, regardless of ascent/apex/descent. `takeoff_left` and `landing_left` affect the hidden legacy sprite, not rig controls. Enlarged frames **3.50,3.625,3.75,3.875,4.00,4.125 s** show essentially the same tucked leg arrangement carried through the arc; **4.25–4.375 s** returns upright without a separately staged landing compression.

**Why it matters:** the root follows gravity while the performance does not express push-off, suspension or absorption. **Minimal correction:** retain responsive jump initiation and existing gravity; author takeoff extension, ascent tuck, apex, descent reach and landing compression using velocity/contact/landing impulse. Do not insert unapproved input latency to obtain anticipation. **Acceptance:** single/double jump, short release, ledge fall and hard/soft landing at real time and six phase frames; support foot leaves/contacts at the correct event, pelvis absorbs landing then settles, and no unchanged airborne pose is simply translated through every phase.

### M5 — Sword combo variations are effects/timing variations over one body trajectory

**Classification:** confirmed source loss of action distinction. **Evidence:** player `:255,328–345,433–446`; rig `:226–231`. Combo_step selects slash/reverse/finisher effects, damage and duration, but the rig receives only `"sword"` and normalized progress for all three. The body always uses the same hand interpolation and weapon rotation; there is no reverse cut, distinct finisher load or return path. No claim of a particular combo being visible in the supplied strip is made.

**Why it matters:** the declared reverse and finisher lack corresponding body mechanics. **Minimal correction:** pass action/variant identity and author three compatible pose trajectories with distinct cut directions and finite recovery; preserve current combo acceptance/balance. **Acceptance:** isolated strikes and full queued combo at normal speed, without slash FX. A reviewer can distinguish first cut, reverse and finisher from silhouette/weapon arc; end pose of each supports entry to the next with no arm teleport.

### M6 — Facing reversal is an instantaneous reflection of the entire pose

**Classification:** confirmed source transform discontinuity; deliberate instant mirroring remains a possible style choice, not automatically a bug. **Evidence:** rig `:163–167`; player `:168–169`; enemy `:115–116,129–137`. The full pivot scale switches sign at once, including sword, feet, garment and asymmetric silhouette; the 90 ms target blend is not a turn transition. Changing facing only injects a small angular spring impulse.

**Why it matters:** for requested full-body weighted turns, the pivot's image reflection cannot show a support step, hip/chest sequence or a weapon reposition. **Minimal correction:** author a short readable side-switch using planted support and view/draw-order replacement; keep input response immediate and let facing/hit authority follow the intended rule. If instant mirroring is retained, explicitly approve it as a limited-animation convention. **Acceptance:** run/brake/reverse and idle turn both directions; inspect last old-facing and first new-facing frames, plus normal-speed readability. Weapon/hands follow an authored route and the support foot does not jump through the root. Rigid single-view cutouts alone cannot supply a convincing front/back perspective turn.

### M7 — Knockback spins the whole cutout while collision and landing remain upright

**Classification:** source-confirmed representation mismatch with a frame-visible rigid tumble; floor penetration severity not established. **Evidence:** enemy `:39–44,142–145,195–213,223–234`; rig `:171,240–245`; Rules `:28–39`. An upright capsule handles translation; external_roll rotates the complete rig about a fixed BodyCenter. Airborne angular velocity has no damping; ground contact rapidly eases the whole pose upright. Death receives only a small torso lean and disappears after 1.3 s. The recording at **0.625–0.875 s** and **1.625–2.00 s** shows whole-body rigid orientations during launch rather than articulated recoil/landing poses.

**Why it matters:** numeric momentum and torque are not sufficient to make a body feel heavy; the silhouette lacks progressive recoil and impact absorption, and visible body orientation can disagree with floor collision. **Minimal correction:** keep arcade impulse magnitude initially; add a small authored recoil/tumble/landing pose set, bounded spin decay and a contact-aware settle/death pose. Validate extreme rotated extents relative to the collider rather than converting to full ragdoll automatically. **Acceptance:** light sword, kick and finisher from both sides; demonstrate hit-frame deformation, airborne arc and first floor-contact frame with collider overlay, then a stable grounded settle. No claim that arbitrary mass/rod equations establish animation realism.

### M8 — Rig construction is rigid cutout hierarchy, with no authored joint/shape limits

**Classification:** confirmed implementation constraint; future pose failure is a hypothesis until exercised. **Evidence:** rig `:122–145,148–155,267–295`. Each part is a four-corner rectangle parented to one Bone2D. There are no mesh bone weights, deforming torso, perspective replacements or per-joint anatomical/art limits. Solver clamps only total reach and fixed bend sign; every limb tip rotation is forced to zero. Pelvis rotation is always zero while torso rotates independently. Correct lengths/pole direction do not certify silhouette, joint contour, boot orientation or believable pelvis/chest counteraction.

**Why it matters:** aggressively extending this rig with more endpoint sine curves is unlikely to solve a full-body martial performance. **Minimal correction:** decide and document the allowed limited side-view range; author joint-angle limits and boot/hand orientations for it. Use overlap/replacement art or deformation only where required; for hero kicks/turns, drawn/replacement poses may be the smaller convincing solution than general procedural anatomy. **Acceptance:** rendered bind/contact/pass/down/up keys and extreme sword/kick/turn silhouettes at final game size, with and without dots. Reject visible seams/collapses and ambiguous limb overlaps; approve whole-body poses before retiming. A solver PASS remains a technical check only.

## Preserve / do not infer

Gravity remains consistently positive-down and applied to velocity via `Rules.fall`; hit impulses add upward launch velocity rather than reversing gravity. That is a useful rule to preserve. It does **not** prove that chosen gravity, launch height, spin or posture feels right. Fixed bend direction and reach clamping are also useful foundations; I did not observe or prove a knee pole flip. Do not report one merely because body movement looks wrong. Camera, effects, sound masking, control latency, full encounter balance and unsupported terrain were not audited.

## Primary references used to prioritize this review

- [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/): explicitly identifies timing, staging, anticipation, follow-through, secondary action, anatomy and weight as performance tools. Supports prioritizing readable phases and body performance; supplies no numerical game timings.
- [Walt Disney Animation Studios — Hand-Drawn Animation](https://disneyanimation.com/process/hand-drawn-animation/): describes frame-specific drawing and weight/mass/overlap. Supports the availability of drawn/replacement action poses, not a mandate to replace every rig.
- [Godot — 2D skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/2d_skeletons.html): distinguishes skeleton/polygon setup from weighted deformation, and explains triangulation/internal vertices for controlled bending. Official page warns it has not yet been updated for 4.7; used only for this conceptual distinction, not a version-specific implementation recipe.
- Richard Williams' official Animator's Survival Kit site was attempted but timed out; no claim from an uninspected book scan or third-party summary is attributed to it.

## Smallest useful next production gate

Choose one body action first: grounded run → brake → right-facing sword contact → recoil → reverse/step. Produce an approved silhouette/phase sheet and a normal-speed, effects-free capture before extending the rest of the move list. Then solve M1 contact ownership and M2 contact timing, validate both directions, and only then develop kick/turn view replacements. This is a production recommendation, not an authorization to change combat mechanics.
