# Three whole-character pixel enemy redraws

The guard, ember keeper and bell wraith now render through `CelActorVisual` using `game/art/pixel/enemies-redraw.png`. Fire and floating enemies no longer use the experimental rigid body-part renderer. Legacy source sprites remain hidden compatibility metadata; experimental rig tools are preserved separately.

The generated atlas has eight authored drawings for each enemy: rest, three gait/hover keys, anticipation, contact, hurt and collapsed defeat. Profiles use the gait keys as a distance-clocked four-record cycle, action keys as a320ms attack and the appropriate drawn hurt/death state. These are sparse motion proofs, not sixty new drawings per second or a finished full animation library. The wraith hover no longer overrides its attack and hurt states.

Packing: `tools/pack_enemy_redraw.py` thresholds alpha at210 to remove the generated translucent matte/glow, uniformly scales each character row, flips left-facing source drawings once to the library's right-facing convention, and registers a fixed support/mask anchor with feet/tail baseline112 on128-square canvases. It does not draw or synthesize poses. Source and native-size contact sheet remain under `assets/art/pixel/enemies-redraw`; `tools/build_enemy_pixel_profiles.py` writes the metadata. Individual attack-contact annotations remain provisional.

Shared gravity, launched hit reactions, floor collision, fire three-bolt fan and wraith hover remain actor mechanics. Damage and windup presentation are red. Enemy footsteps now request `enemy_step` independently of player steps. The new regression verifies all three active visual types, distinct attack/hurt states, nearest-neighbor filtering, grounded walkers and hovering wraith. `enemy_pixel_visuals`, `body_physics` and `kick_states` pass. A concurrent `recovery_flow` alpha-flash assertion conflicts with the new red player-feedback style and was reported to the parent for update; it is not an enemy test pass.

References: [Saint11: Consistency](https://saint11.art/blog/consistency/) discusses pixel scale/palette consistency; [Godot sprite animation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_sprite_animation.html) explains authored SpriteFrames playback. Those principles do not establish subjective motion quality. The contact sheet was inspected as still frames, not continuous audiovisual playback. Normal-speed motion and human art approval remain open.
